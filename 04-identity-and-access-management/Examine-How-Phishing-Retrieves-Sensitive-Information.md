# Examine How Phishing Retrieves Sensitive Information

## Overview

Email attacks are one of the most common ways hackers gain access to organizational networks. Attackers often target users through:

- Phishing
- Spoofing
- Malware
- Credential theft

Once attackers compromise:
- A user account
- A device
- Login credentials

they can threaten the security of the entire organization.

---

# What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack technique used to:

- Trick users into revealing sensitive information. Phishing is one of the most common cyberattack methods used to steal credentials, sensitive data, and install malware. Attackers often use legitimate-looking emails, fake websites, and social engineering techniques to deceive users. Malware payloads delivered through phishing attacks can include viruses, trojans, rootkits, and spyware. Organizations can reduce phishing risks through user education, multifactor authentication, email protection, and layered Microsoft 365 security controls.

Examples include:
- Usernames
- Passwords
- Credit card numbers
- Banking information
- Personal data

---

# How Phishing Works

Attackers send emails that:
- Appear legitimate
- Pretend to come from trusted organizations
- Create urgency
- Encourage users to click malicious links

---

# Common Phishing Scenarios

Phishing emails may appear to come from:

- Banks
- Microsoft
- IT administrators
- Delivery companies
- Online services
- Executives
- Trusted vendors

---

# Typical Phishing Attack Flow

1. User receives a fake email.
2. Email appears legitimate.
3. User clicks a malicious link.
4. User visits a fake website.
5. User enters credentials or downloads malware.
6. Attackers steal data or infect the device.

---

# Characteristics of Phishing Emails

Phishing emails commonly include:

- Fake branding
- Suspicious URLs
- Urgent language
- Threats or warnings
- Fake login pages
- Unexpected attachments
